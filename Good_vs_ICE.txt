## LEGAL FRAMEWORK - SELF DEFENSE

### General Self-Defense Requirements
1. **Reasonable belief** of danger (objective reasonable person standard)
2. **Imminent threat** (immediate and unavoidable)
3. **Proportional response** (force matches threat level)
4. **No excessive force** (stop when threat ends)

### State Variations
- **Duty to retreat**: Some states require retreat before deadly force
- **Stand your ground**: Other states eliminate duty to retreat
- **Castle doctrine**: Enhanced self-defense rights in home

### Law Enforcement Standard: Graham v. Connor
- Uses "objectively reasonable" standard
- Evaluated from "perspective of a reasonable officer on the scene"
- Considers split-second judgments under pressure
- Does NOT require perfect decision-making
- Broader authority than civilian self-defense
- Must still meet reasonableness requirements

### Deadly Force Justification (From Image)
Four scenarios allowing deadly force:
1. You are in imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury
2. A person in your presence is in imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury
3. You are having a felony committed upon your person
4. A felony is being committed inside your home or abode

## LEGAL ARGUMENTS - AGAINST JUSTIFICATION

### Creating the Danger
- Did Ross approach vehicle in way that put himself at unnecessary risk?
- Given his prior trauma, should he have positioned differently?
- Should he have maintained distance from vehicle?
- Did his approach CREATE the circumstances requiring deadly force?

### Objective vs. Subjective Threat
- Trauma explains WHY he perceived threat
- Legal standard: "reasonable given the facts" not "understandable given psychology"
- Subjective fear must match objective reality
- Was threat objectively real or transitional vehicle motion?

### Car Turning Away
- Video may show car already steering right/away
- Brief forward movement may be transitional, not threatening
- Question: Was vehicle actually aimed at officer?
- Duration was "incredibly incredibly brief"

### Other Officers' Reactions
- Second agent in close proximity did not fire
- Suggests alternative threat assessments were possible
- What would "reasonable officer" standard conclude?
- Why didn't other officer perceive same threat level?

### Elevated Threat Perception Due to Trauma
- Does prior trauma make someone LESS objective?
- Seeing threats that aren't objectively there
- Should traumatized officers be removed from similar situations?
- Does trauma excuse unreasonable perception?

### Holding Phone During Shooting
- Policing experts questioned holding phone while shooting
- Criminology professor Geoff Alpert: "want to see the officer training that permits" this
- Suggests lack of proper training or protocol adherence
- One-handed shooting while filming = questionable tactics

### Automaticity Under Threat
- Actions occur without conscious deliberation
- Body responds faster than mind can think
- Evidence: Phone not dropped, immediate firing
- Cannot be easily controlled or stopped once triggered

### "Limited RAM" Framework
- Single-threaded attention in humans
- Cannot fully process multiple threats

### Tragedy of Convergence
- Good: Flight mode, tunnel vision, escape focus
- Ross: Attention diverted, trauma-primed, split-second perception

- Result: Catastrophic intersection of human psychological limitations

### Legal vs. Psychological Distinction
- Psychological analysis explains WHAT happened and WHY
- Legal analysis asks: Was it JUSTIFIED?
- Understanding mechanism ≠ legal justification
- Empathy ≠ legal reasonableness
- Courts must separate comprehensibility from legality
